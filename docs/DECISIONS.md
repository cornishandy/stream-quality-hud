# Decisions

Why the HUD works the way it does. Newest first.

## 1.7.0 (21 Oct 2026): data grade, overlays, sound the player picks
- **Data grade** (Andy: the bitrate ranges said "Normal" without saying how good). Bits per pixel = video bitrate ÷ (width × height × frame rate), then adjusted for codec (H.264 1, HEVC/VP9/Dolby Vision 1.6, AV1/VVC 2) and size (fourth root of the area against 1080p). Bands: A ≥ 0.30 disc-like, B ≥ 0.15 generous, C ≥ 0.09 typical streaming, D ≥ 0.055 lean, F starved. A typical 1080p Blu-ray (H.264, about 30 Mbps at 24 fps) scores about 0.6, which gives the "% of a Blu-ray's data" figure. The factors and bands are a rough guide (Opus's judgement, not a measurement), and the panel says so. HLS figures cover the video plus its largest sound pairing (RFC 8216), so the largest listed sound bitrate is taken off when every sound track lists one; otherwise the grade allows up to 768 kbps of sound and can read as two letters ("D or F"). When several bitrates are listed at the playing size and the exact one isn't known (Safari playing a master list itself), the low end is graded too, so it reads "B to D" with "up to" on the figures (Claude Fable 5.1's final gate, round 1).
- **Overlays.** The HUD lists what the page draws over the video (shading, tints, solid layers, subtitles, controls, canvases, images) and any filter, opacity or blend applied to the video or the elements that hold it. Stacking is approximated by position and z-index under the shared ancestor, then page order; coverage uses the picture's own rectangle, not letterbox bars; darkness counts the opacity of the layers holding a shade, and light tints are called light. When nothing is found it says a player can hide layers from this check, since closed shadow roots and unusual colour spaces can't be read. GPT-6.1-Sol's review (round 1, NO-GO) found: layers behind the video, letterbox bars, opacity and light tints, missed pseudo-element and color() layers, the HLS sound share, a frame rate from the wrong version, and a sound line that blamed the player when the playing track was unknown. All are fixed. Its HLS harness expects an exact letter; the HUD gives a range instead when the list doesn't say the sound's bitrate. `elementsFromPoint` isn't used because it skips `pointer-events: none` layers, which is how most shades are drawn. A filter or a solid layer is Unusual; a shade of 25% or darker is a Note.
- **Chrome looking darker than Safari is not a layer.** An earlier answer said Chrome "adds a layer". GPT-6.1-Sol's research (21 Oct 2026) rejected that: both browsers sometimes hand video to a Core Animation layer and sometimes convert it themselves. The likelier cause is the colour conversion: Chromium's source notes that "Apple's CoreVideo uses gamma=1.961", while Chromium's ordinary BT.709 conversion uses "the same transfer function as sRGB". A page can't see which path runs, so the panel lists it under "Can't tell".
- **Lines don't bounce** (Andy: rows appearing and disappearing made the open panel jump). While the panel shows one video, a line once shown keeps its place (reading "—" when it has nothing now), each line keeps the tallest it has been, and the panel never shrinks by itself. So a line can push the others down once, the first time it appears, and nothing moves after that. A new video, closing the panel, or opening or closing Details starts afresh (Claude Fable 5.1, final gate round 2, found the panel kept its old height on a new video; fixed). "Over the video" always shows, reading "nothing found" when empty.
- **The player picks the sound track.** A better track in the stream list used to be ▲ with "Pick … in the player's audio menu". Web players' audio menus pick the language, not the format (Peacock's is "Audio & Subtitle"), and Dolby's own guidance says a provider "may choose to only deliver the Atmos experience to capable devices". So it is now – with "<service>'s player chose it over <track>", and no step. Chrome on a Mac can't decode E-AC-3 at all (Chromium builds the AC-3/E-AC-3 decoder for Cast and tvOS, not Mac); Safari can, including Atmos (JOC).

## 1.6.0 (19–20 Oct 2026): compact scorecard
- **F2 Compact scorecard**, chosen by Andy from 5 mockups and 2 finalists judged by GPT-6.1-Sol, GPT-6-Astra, Grok 4.7 and Gemini 4 Argon.
- **Five marks:** ▲ better on this Mac, ◇ worth trying, – top sent, ✓ computer best, ? unknown. Shape and colour together; the row text carries the words.
- **Pill rules:** shown for a known service, or a video of 10 minutes or more; fades after 3 seconds of idle while the video plays.
- **Accuracy rules:** no "pixel for pixel", no "TV apps only", and the 60 Hz line names a Safari setting worth trying rather than a claim that the Mac holds the video back.
- **Round 1 corrections:** timing runs only while the pill or panel is connected; cold Smoothness reads “measuring…” without a judgement. Unestablished limits read “unknown”, not a claim that the service publishes nothing. Prime Video’s unnumbered HD needs 1080p for ✓; mono ranks below stereo. Details names the stream list and the browser receiving it.
- **Final gate (Claude Fable 5.1):** the unnumbered-HD rule applies only when no stream list says more, so a 720p film whose Prime Video list goes to 1080p is still flagged; a mouse click leaves the pill unfocused, so it can fade and Space still reaches the player.

## 1.5.1 (19 Oct 2026): shorter wording
- **Headings are 2 to 5 words, under 36 characters,** with the words that matter first and no filler: "Best for Safari", "Picture below best". Notes and lines get the same treatment. The panel had grown wordy enough to lose its meaning.
- **"Elsewhere", not "Better".** The service's best anywhere (say, 4K HDR in its TV apps) read as if this film was on offer in 4K. The line now starts "Elsewhere:" and ends "not every title".
- **Refresh says whose limit it is.** "Safari's page limit, not the video's" replaces "draws pages at 60 Hz to save power", which read as the Mac holding playback back.

## 1.5 (18 Oct 2026): judged rows and a verdict, not raw numbers
- **A verdict headline replaces the green status text.** The old line ("Best size…") coloured a technical fact green, which didn't answer the real question: is this the best I can get here, and if not, where? The verdict now says that in words, worst problem first: not the best picture yet → best picture but not the best sound → best this browser gets, more elsewhere → best this site offers → can't confirm.
- **A cue dot carries the colour, not the text.** It sits in the top-right corner, where a projectionist's reel-change mark appears. Sage means best here, blue means more exists elsewhere, amber means not the best, and a hollow ring means can't confirm.
- **Every row is judged Normal, Unusual or Note, with a reason.** Only exceptions get colour (amber for Unusual), so a normal stream reads calm.
- **Sound got its own section:** the playing track (WebKit's track configuration, or the downloaded audio matched to the list), the list's best track and whether this browser says it can decode it, and each service's published sound limits.
- **"After this" row:** the HUD sits before any audio processing on the Mac, so it says what comes next (Movie Sound, a Chrome audio extension) and that it can't see it.
- **Refresh vs smoothness.** Safari draws web pages at about 60 Hz even on a 120 Hz screen (the video itself is drawn natively), while Chrome reports the screen's real rate. So "Refresh" is a note, and "Smoothness" is measured from `requestVideoFrameCallback` frame times (even or uneven pacing). WebKit reports when frames are due, not when they were shown, so there the HUD says "can't check here" rather than guess.

## 1.3–1.4: Safari, published caps, refresh rate
- **Safari runs it as a userscript** through the free Userscripts app. A real Safari extension needs Xcode and a signing identity, and a temporary developer extension disappears when Safari quits. `hud.js` already works without extension APIs, so the userscript is the same file with a header.
- **Published caps per service** (Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+, Hulu, Max, Apple TV+, Paramount+, Peacock, AMC+, MUBI, Criterion Channel), taken from each service's own help pages, with the source and quote for each in `SERVICES.md` and `SOUND.md`. Where a service publishes no browser cap, the HUD says so instead of inventing one.
- **Orion** (a WebKit browser that presents Chrome's user agent to extensions) is detected by WebKit-only APIs and named as Orion, with no service caps, since no service publishes one for it.

## 1.0–1.2: exact evidence only
Two independent code reviews (GPT-6.1-Sol, NO-GO twice) shaped these rules:
- **No background requests, no host permissions.** An early version fetched stream lists again from the extension's background, with access to every site. That's a privacy and security risk, so it was removed. Now the HUD only re-reads lists the page itself already downloaded, from inside the page with the page's own rights (usually from the browser's cache).
- **A verdict needs the video's own list.** That's either its HLS source, or the list whose segments match the video's newest downloads since it started playing. A trailer's list, a hidden second video, trick-play tracks or a multi-period DASH list can't certify "best".
- **The HUD never touches audio.** It creates no AudioContext and doesn't read channel counts through Web Audio, so turning it on or off can't change what you hear.
- **Toggle is Control+Shift+H.** Control+Option+Shift combinations are commonly taken by window managers.
- **A bare `<video>` in full screen can't show the panel.** Nothing can draw over it. Sites that put their whole player in full screen (most do) are fine.
